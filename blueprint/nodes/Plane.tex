\begin{definition}[The ambient plane]
Let $\mathrm{Plane}$ denote two-dimensional Euclidean space $\mathbb{R}^2$: the real vector
space of functions $x\colon\{1,2\}\to\mathbb{R}$, equipped with the standard inner product
$\langle x,y\rangle=x_1y_1+x_2y_2$, the norm $\lvert x\rvert=\langle x,x\rangle^{1/2}$ that
this inner product induces, and the Lebesgue measure determined by that Euclidean structure.

Every function, domain, and ball appearing in this development lives in $\mathrm{Plane}$.
In particular, for $x_0\in\mathrm{Plane}$ and $r>0$ we write
$B_r(x_0)=\{y\in\mathrm{Plane}:\lvert y-x_0\rvert<r\}$ for the open Euclidean ball, and
$B_r:=B_r(0)$, as in the paper's convention $B_r=B_r(0)\subset\mathbb{R}^2$.
\end{definition}
